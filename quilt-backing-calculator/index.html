How to use this calculator

  1. Choose inches or centimeters and enter your finished quilt width and length.
  2. Set the extra per side. Four inches (10 cm) on every edge is the usual allowance for quilting and squaring up.
  3. Pick your fabric width: standard 42–44 in cotton (40 in usable after trimming selvedges) or 108 in wide backing (104 in usable).
  4. Press Calculate to compare vertical and horizontal seam layouts.
  5. Buy the recommended yardage, which is rounded up to the nearest quarter yard.

The formula

backing W = quilt W + 2 × extra backing L = quilt L + 2 × extra // vertical seams panels = ceil(backing W ÷ usable width) · yards = panels × backing L ÷ 36 // horizontal seams panels = ceil(backing L ÷ usable width) · yards = panels × backing W ÷ 36 round up to the nearest ¼ yard
Worked exampleA 60 × 80 in quilt with 4 in extra per side needs a 68 × 88 in back. On 40 in usable fabric: vertical seams = 2 panels × 88 ÷ 36 = 4.89 → 5 yd; horizontal seams = 3 panels × 68 ÷ 36 = 5.67 → 5¾ yd. The vertical layout is cheaper.

Why backing needs to be bigger than the quilt top

The quilt back must be larger than the top so there is room to load it on a longarm frame, to clamp it during hand quilting, and to allow for “draw-up” — the slight shrinking that happens as quilting stitches pull the layers together. Four inches on each side is the standard allowance; use 2–3 inches for a domestic machine and up to 6 inches if you are sending the quilt to a professional quilter.

Wide backing or standard cotton?

Wide-backing fabric, around 108 inches, lets you use a single piece for most quilts up to queen size, with no seams to match. It often costs more per yard, but you buy fewer yards. Standard quilting cotton is more widely available in prints, but needs one or two seams. For small quilts such as baby and lap sizes, standard cotton is usually cheaper; for queen and king quilts, wide backing is generally the better value.

Seam placement tips

  • Press seams open to reduce bulk and help the needle glide.
  • Trim away selvedges before joining panels; they shrink differently and can pucker.
  • A centre seam is the most common, but an off-centre seam is often easier to line up on a longarm.

Frequently asked questions

How much fabric do I need for quilt backing?

Add 8 inches to the quilt width and length (4 in per side), then divide by the usable fabric width to find the number of panels. Multiply by the backing length in yards. The calculator does it for you and rounds up to the nearest ¼ yard.

What is the usable width of quilting fabric?

Standard quilting cotton is sold at 42–44 in wide, but about 40 in is usable once the selvedges are trimmed. Wide backing at 108 in gives roughly 104 in usable.

Should I run the seam vertically or horizontally?

Choose whichever needs fewer yards, as shown by the calculator. A vertical seam usually saves fabric on tall quilts; a horizontal seam can save fabric on very wide ones.

How much extra backing do I need for a longarm quilter?

Most longarmers ask for 4–6 inches extra on each side. Check with your quilter before you buy.

Do I need to pre-wash the backing fabric?

It’s a good idea. Cotton can shrink 2–5% in the wash, so buy a little extra if you pre-wash.

Can I use the calculator in metric?

Yes. Switch to centimeters and enter your sizes in cm. The calculator converts to inches internally and shows the result in yards and meters.

Why round up to the nearest quarter yard?

Fabric shops usually cut in ¼ yard increments, so rounding up makes sure you buy enough fabric.
