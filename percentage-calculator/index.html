Percentage Calculator

Pick the question you want answered, enter two numbers, and get the percentage straight away — no formulas to remember.

How to use this calculator

  1. Choose the type of question from the drop-down, such as “What is A% of B?”.
  2. Type your first number in A and the second in B, matching the wording of your question.
  3. Press Calculate to see the answer and the calculation behind it.
  4. Change the mode to answer a different question using the same numbers.

The formula

A% of B = A ÷ 100 × B A is what % of B = A ÷ B × 100 % change = (B − A) ÷ A × 100 add B% to A = A × (1 + B ÷ 100) subtract B% = A × (1 − B ÷ 100)
Worked example15% of 240 = 15 ÷ 100 × 240 = 36. A price rising from 80 to 100 is a change of (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = +25%. Adding 20% to 50 gives 50 × 1.2 = 60.

Percentage change vs. percentage points

If an interest rate rises from 4% to 5%, it has gone up by one percentage point, but the rate itself has increased by 25% (1 ÷ 4). Mixing the two up is a very common error in news stories and spreadsheets. Use the “percentage change” mode when you compare two amounts, and describe rate differences in percentage points.

Why a 20% rise and a 20% fall do not cancel

Increase 100 by 20% and you reach 120. Reduce 120 by 20% and you get 96, not 100. The percentage applies to a different starting number each time. To undo a 20% increase you need a fall of about 16.7%.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Divide the percentage by 100 and multiply by the number. For 15% of 240: 15 ÷ 100 × 240 = 36.

How do I work out a percentage increase?

Subtract the old value from the new one, divide by the old value, and multiply by 100.

How do I add a percentage to a number?

Multiply the number by 1 plus the percentage as a decimal. To add 20%, multiply by 1.2.

How do I find what percent one number is of another?

Divide the first number by the second and multiply by 100.

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

Percentage points are the arithmetic difference between two percentages. Percent change compares them proportionally.

Can percentages be over 100%?

Yes. A value that is double the original is a 100% increase, or 200% of the original.

Why can’t I divide by zero?

Percent change is relative to the starting number, so a start of zero has no defined percentage.
