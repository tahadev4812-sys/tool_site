Image Compressor

Make pictures smaller for email, websites and uploads. Choose a quality, see the savings straight away, and download — no upload needed.

How to use this tool

  1. Add your images by dragging them in or clicking the box.
  2. Choose JPG or WebP output, then set the quality (70–80% suits most web use).
  3. Optionally enter a maximum width, such as 1600 px, to shrink very large photos even more.
  4. Compare the before and after sizes, then download your files.

How compression saves space

1. quality → lower quality, smaller file 2. dimensions → fewer pixels, smaller file phone photo: 4000 × 3000 px, 4 MB → max width 1600 px, quality 75% → about 250 KB
Two leversA 4 MB phone photo resized to 1600 px wide at 75% quality usually lands between 200 and 400 KB — roughly 90% smaller — and still looks sharp on a screen.

Smaller images, faster pages

Large pictures are the most common reason web pages load slowly. Compressing images before you publish them improves speed, helps search rankings and saves visitors’ mobile data. For email and forms, it helps you stay under attachment limits.

PNG files

PNG is lossless, so there is no quality slider that can shrink it. To compress a PNG photo, convert it to JPG or WebP here; flat graphics with few colours are better left as PNG.

Larger result?

Occasionally a file that is already heavily optimised gets bigger when re-encoded. If that happens, the tool tells you — lower the quality or keep the original.

Frequently asked questions

How do I reduce the size of an image?

Add it to the compressor, lower the quality a little and optionally set a maximum width. Download the smaller version.

What is the best quality setting?

For the web, 75–80% balances size and clarity. Go higher for printing.

Will compression make my photo blurry?

At moderate settings the loss is not visible. Very low quality causes blockiness around edges.

Can I compress PNG files?

PNG is lossless, so convert PNG photos to JPG or WebP for large savings.

Is my image uploaded?

No. Compression happens in your browser.

How do I get an image under 100 KB?

Set a maximum width of 800–1000 px and quality of about 60–70%, then check the result and adjust.

Does compressing remove metadata?

Yes. Re-encoding drops EXIF data such as camera details and location, which also helps privacy.
