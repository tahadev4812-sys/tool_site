How to use this calculator

  1. Choose imperial or metric units, then enter the slab length and width.
  2. Enter the thickness. Four inches (10 cm) is standard for patios and paths.
  3. Keep the 10% waste allowance, or raise it for uneven ground.
  4. Press Calculate to see cubic yards, cubic meters and the number of bags.
  5. For anything over about 1.5 cubic yards, ask a ready-mix supplier for a quote.

Cubic yards: the unit concrete is sold in

In the US and Canada, ready-mix concrete is ordered by the cubic yard — a cube three feet on each side, equal to 27 cubic feet. UK and Australian suppliers use cubic meters, which is why this calculator reports both. Because delivery trucks usually have a minimum order, it often makes sense to order slightly more rather than risk running short during the pour.

Choose the right thickness

Thickness determines strength and cost. A 4 inch slab suits footpaths, patios and shed bases. Garage floors and driveways that carry vehicles are usually 5–6 inches. Heavy loads need engineering advice and steel reinforcement.

  • Compact the sub-base before pouring.
  • Use reinforcement mesh or rebar for driveways and large slabs.
  • Cut control joints to guide cracking.

Bagged mix or ready-mix?

One 80 lb bag makes about 0.6 cubic feet. A cubic yard therefore needs 45 bags — about 3,600 lb to lift and mix by hand. For small repairs, fence posts and steps, bags are ideal. For larger slabs, a delivered load saves time and gives a more consistent mix.

Frequently asked questions

How much concrete do I need for a 10 × 10 slab?

At 4 inches thick, a 10 × 10 ft slab needs 33.3 cubic feet, or about 1.23 cubic yards. With 10% extra, order about 1.36 cubic yards.

How many 80 lb bags of concrete are in a yard?

About 45 bags of 80 lb mix make one cubic yard, because each bag yields roughly 0.6 cubic feet.

How thick should a concrete patio be?

Four inches is standard for patios, paths and shed bases. Driveways usually need 5–6 inches.

Do I need extra concrete for waste?

Yes, 5–10% extra is normal because of spillage, uneven ground and slightly over-dug sub-bases.

How do I convert cubic feet to cubic yards?

Divide the number of cubic feet by 27, since one cubic yard equals 27 cubic feet.

Can I calculate in metric?

Yes. Switch to metric and enter meters for length and width and centimeters for thickness. Results include cubic meters.

Does this include the gravel base?

No. Calculate gravel separately; a 4 inch compacted base is typical under slabs.
