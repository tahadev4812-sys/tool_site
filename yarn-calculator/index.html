How to use this calculator

  1. Make a gauge swatch (about 4 × 4 in or 10 × 10 cm) in the stitch pattern and hook or needle size you plan to use.
  2. Measure the swatch width and height, then work out the yards used — unravel it and measure, or weigh it and compare to the skein weight.
  3. Pick a blanket preset or type your own project width and height. Use the same unit for the swatch and the project.
  4. Enter the yards per skein from the label and keep the 10% extra allowance.
  5. Press Calculate to see the total yardage and number of skeins to buy.

The formula

yards per square unit = swatch yards ÷ (swatch width × swatch height) total yards = yards per square unit × project width × project height × (1 + extra% ÷ 100) skeins = ceil( total yards ÷ yards per skein ) meters = yards × 0.9144
Worked exampleA 4 × 4 in swatch uses 8 yards, so each square inch needs 8 ÷ 16 = 0.5 yd. A 50 × 60 in throw is 3,000 in², so it needs 1,500 yd; add 10% = 1,650 yards. With 220 yd skeins: 1,650 ÷ 220 = 7.5 → 8 skeins.

Why the swatch method is the most accurate

Rules of thumb such as “a throw needs 1,500 yards” ignore the things that actually decide yardage: your tension, your hook or needle size, and the stitch pattern. A dense single crochet fabric can use almost twice as much yarn per square inch as an open mesh. By measuring a real swatch in the exact stitch you will use, the calculator scales up your own consumption instead of guessing.

How to measure yarn used in a swatch

The easiest method is to weigh it. Weigh your swatch on a kitchen scale, then divide by the weight of a full skein and multiply by the skein’s yardage. For example, a swatch weighing 10 g from a 100 g, 220 yd skein used 10 ÷ 100 × 220 = 22 yd. If you do not own a scale, unravel the swatch and measure the strand against a tape measure.

How much extra yarn should I buy?

The default 10% covers weaving in ends, joining new skeins and small differences between swatch and finished fabric. Go higher (15–20%) for lace, cables or colour-work, which consume more yarn. Always buy from one dye lot: shades vary between batches and a visible stripe halfway up a blanket is hard to hide.

Frequently asked questions

How much yarn do I need for a blanket?

It depends on the size, yarn weight and stitch. As a rough guide, a worsted-weight baby blanket takes about 600 yd, a throw about 1,650 yd and a queen about 5,000 yd. A gauge swatch gives a far more reliable figure.

How do I find yards per skein?

Look on the yarn label; it is printed as yards and meters, for example “220 yd / 201 m”. Yards per skein differ widely between brands and weights.

Does the calculator work for knitting and crochet?

Yes. The swatch method works for any stitch, in knitting or crochet, as long as you use the same stitch pattern for the swatch and the project.

Can I use centimeters?

Yes. Switch to centimeters and enter your swatch and project sizes in cm. The yardage remains in yards, with the metric total shown in meters.

What if my swatch is not 4 inches?

Any size works. The calculator divides the yards used by the swatch area, so a 3 × 5 in swatch is just as valid as a 4 × 4 in one — but bigger swatches are more reliable.

How many skeins do I need for a scarf?

A typical scarf of 8 × 60 in uses about 240–400 yards of worsted yarn, so usually 2 skeins. Enter your swatch and scarf size for an exact number.

Should I round up the number of skeins?

Always. The calculator rounds up automatically, so you never end up one partial skein short.
