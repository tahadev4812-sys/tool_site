Choosing JPG quality

QualityUse it forResult
90–100%Printing, archivingNear-original, larger file
80–90%Photos you share or postLooks identical, much smaller
60–75%Websites and emailSmall, minor detail loss
Below 50%Thumbnails onlyVisible blockiness

JPG works best for photos. Sharp text and flat colours can show fuzzy edges at low quality.

Why convert PNG to JPG?

PNG is perfect for graphics but heavy for photographs. A phone screenshot or a photo saved as PNG can be many megabytes, which slows websites, fills up storage and exceeds email and form-upload limits. JPG uses lossy compression to make the same picture a fraction of the size, which is usually the quickest way to get under a size limit.

Watch out for transparency and text

If your PNG has a transparent background, it will turn white. For screenshots full of small text, keep quality high (90%+) or stay with PNG, as heavy JPG compression can make letters look smudged.

No uploads

The conversion runs in your browser, so screenshots and personal pictures are never sent anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert PNG to JPG?

Add the PNG file to the box, choose a quality, and download the JPG once it appears in the list.

Will the PNG’s transparent background be kept?

No. JPG does not support transparency, so transparent areas become white.

What quality should I choose?

85–90% is a good default. Use 70–80% if you need a much smaller file.

Why is my JPG smaller than the PNG?

JPG discards fine detail the eye rarely notices, whereas PNG keeps every pixel.

Can I convert many PNGs together?

Yes. Add several files and download them all at once.

Is converting PNG to JPG reversible?

Not fully. Converting back to PNG will not restore detail lost during JPG compression, so keep your original.

Are my files private?

Yes. Nothing leaves your device.
