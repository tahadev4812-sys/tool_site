How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the original price before any discount.
  2. Type the percentage off shown on the sale tag.
  3. If you have a coupon, add it as an extra discount; it is applied after the first.
  4. Enter your sales tax or VAT to see the price you actually pay, then press Calculate.

The formula

sale price = price × (1 − discount% ÷ 100) with extra = sale price × (1 − extra% ÷ 100) tax = price after discounts × tax% ÷ 100 final = price after discounts + tax
Worked exampleA 120 jacket at 25% off = 120 × 0.75 = 90. A further 10% coupon makes it 81. With 8% tax: 81 × 1.08 = 87.48. You save 39 before tax.

How stacked discounts really work

When a store offers “20% off, then an extra 10% off”, the second discount applies to the already-reduced price. The total saving is 28%, not 30%. This calculator applies them in sequence, matching how checkouts do it, so you can see the true price before buying.

Tax, VAT and GST

Tax is normally added after discounts. In the US, sales tax varies by state and city; in the UK, VAT is usually already included in the shelf price; in Canada and Australia, GST (and in Canada PST or HST) is added at the till. Enter your local rate to see the figure you will actually pay.

Is the discount a real bargain?

  • Compare the sale price with the price at other shops.
  • Be wary of inflated “original” prices.
  • Factor in delivery fees and returns policies.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a percentage discount?

Multiply the price by the discount percentage divided by 100 to get the saving, then subtract it from the price. Or multiply by (1 − discount/100) in one step.

How much is 30% off 80?

30% of 80 is 24, so the sale price is 56.

Do two discounts add together?

No. A second discount applies to the reduced price, so 20% and 10% together equal 28% off.

Is tax added before or after the discount?

Usually after: the discount reduces the price first, then tax applies to the lower amount. Check your local rules.

How do I find the original price from the sale price?

Divide the sale price by (1 − discount/100). If an item costs 75 after 25% off, the original price was 75 ÷ 0.75 = 100.

What is the percentage saved?

Savings divided by the original price, times 100. The calculator shows it for you.

Does this work in any currency?

Yes. Amounts have no symbol, so use your own currency.
