How to use this calculator

  1. Choose imperial or metric units.
  2. Enter your height (feet and inches, or centimeters).
  3. Enter your weight.
  4. Press Calculate to see your BMI, category and healthy weight range.

The formula

metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)² imperial: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)² healthy weight range = 18.5 × h² to 24.9 × h² (h in meters)
Worked exampleA person who is 175 cm (1.75 m) and weighs 70 kg: BMI = 70 ÷ 1.75² = 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.9, in the healthy range of 18.5–24.9.

These cut-offs apply to adults aged 20 and over. Children use age- and sex-specific percentile charts.

What BMI can and cannot tell you

Body mass index compares weight to height and is widely used to screen for weight-related health risks across populations. It is quick and free, but it cannot distinguish muscle from fat, does not show where fat is stored and may be less accurate for athletes, older adults, pregnant women and people of some ethnic backgrounds. Use it as one data point, alongside waist measurement, fitness and blood tests.

Using the healthy range

The range shown is the weight that gives a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 at your height. It is a guide rather than a target; the healthiest weight for you depends on your age, build and medical history. If you want to lose or gain weight, talk with a doctor or registered dietitian.

Not for children

This calculator is for adults. Children and teenagers are assessed using BMI-for-age percentiles that account for growth, so use a paediatric growth chart or ask your doctor.

Frequently asked questions

How is BMI calculated?

Divide weight in kilograms by height in meters squared. In imperial units, multiply weight in pounds by 703 and divide by height in inches squared.

What is a healthy BMI?

For adults, the WHO defines a healthy weight as a BMI of 18.5 to 24.9.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not always. Muscle weighs more than fat, so muscular people may have a high BMI without excess body fat.

Does BMI differ for men and women?

The adult categories are the same, though body fat percentages at a given BMI differ between men and women.

Can I use BMI for my child?

No. Children use age- and sex-specific percentile charts. Ask your doctor.

Does age affect BMI?

The formula does not change, but a slightly higher BMI may be acceptable in older adults. Ask your doctor.

Is this medical advice?

No. It is an informational screening tool. Please consult a health professional about your weight.
