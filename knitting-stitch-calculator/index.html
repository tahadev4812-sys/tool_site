How to use this calculator

  1. Knit a swatch at least 6 in (15 cm) wide in the stitch you will use, then block it as you would the finished piece.
  2. Count the stitches and rows across a section and measure the width and height they cover.
  3. Enter the finished size you want.
  4. If your stitch pattern repeats every few stitches, enter the pattern multiple; add any edge stitches.
  5. Press Calculate to see how many stitches to cast on and rows to knit. For crochet, use it for the foundation chain.

The formula

stitches per unit = swatch stitches ÷ swatch width rows per unit = swatch rows ÷ swatch height stitches needed = stitches per unit × finished width cast on = round((needed − edge) ÷ multiple) × multiple + edge rows = ceil(rows per unit × finished length)
Worked exampleA 4 in swatch has 20 stitches and 28 rows, so gauge is 5 sts and 7 rows per inch. For a 50 × 60 in blanket: 5 × 50 = 250 stitches, with 2 edge stitches and a multiple of 1 → cast on 250. Rows: 7 × 60 = 420 rows.

Why you should always knit a swatch

Two knitters using the same yarn and needles can end up with different sizes, because tension is personal. A swatch measures your tension, so the stitch count is based on how you really knit. Even a half-stitch difference per inch adds up to several inches across a blanket or a sweater.

Pattern multiples and edge stitches

Many stitch patterns — ribbing, cables, lace — repeat every few stitches, and your cast-on must fit a whole number of repeats. The calculator rounds to the nearest multiple, then adds the edge stitches that sit outside the repeat. If you want a slightly larger piece, increase the width by one repeat rather than by a single stitch.

Using it for crochet

The same maths works for crochet: count stitches and rows in a swatch, then use the result for the number of foundation chains and rows. Remember to add the turning chain your pattern calls for.

Frequently asked questions

How many stitches should I cast on?

Multiply your stitches per inch (from a gauge swatch) by the finished width in inches, then adjust for your pattern’s stitch multiple and edge stitches. The calculator does this for you.

How big should a gauge swatch be?

At least 6 inches (15 cm) square, measured in the middle where the stitches are most even. Larger is more accurate.

What is a stitch multiple?

The number of stitches in one repeat of a stitch pattern. If a pattern repeats every 4 stitches, your cast-on must be a multiple of 4, plus any edge stitches.

Does this work for crochet?

Yes. Count stitches and rows in a crochet swatch and use the result as the foundation chain and row count.

Should I measure the swatch before or after blocking?

After. Washing and blocking can change the gauge, so measure it the way the finished piece will be treated.

What if my swatch does not match the pattern gauge?

Change needle size: use larger needles if you have too many stitches per inch and smaller if you have too few.

How do I calculate how many rows to knit?

Divide the rows in your swatch by its height to get rows per inch, then multiply by the finished length.
