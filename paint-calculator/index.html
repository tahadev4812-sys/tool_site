How to use this calculator

  1. Pick feet or meters and measure the length, width and height of the room.
  2. Enter the number of doors and windows — their area is subtracted from the walls.
  3. Choose how many coats you plan to apply. Two coats is standard for a colour change.
  4. Tick ceiling if you are painting it too, and check the coverage printed on the can.
  5. Press Calculate and buy the number of cans shown (rounded up).

The formula

wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height − doors × 20 − windows × 15 ceiling area = length × width // optional paint (gal) = (wall + ceiling area) × coats ÷ coverage per gallon liters = gallons × 3.785
Worked exampleA 14 × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls, 1 door and 2 windows: 2 × (14 + 12) × 8 = 416 sq ft, minus 20 and 30 = 366 sq ft. Two coats = 732 sq ft. At 350 sq ft per gallon that is 2.09 gallons, so buy 3 one-gallon cans (or two gallons and a quart).

Why paint estimates are often wrong

Eyeballing the amount of paint usually leads to either a second trip to the store or a garage full of half-used cans. The most common mistakes are forgetting to count two coats, ignoring the ceiling and trusting a coverage figure that does not match your surface. Smooth, primed drywall reaches the full coverage on the can; rough stucco, textured plaster or porous bare wood can use up to 20% more.

Primer, colour changes and dark shades

A primer coat evens out porosity and blocks stains, so the topcoat goes further. Moving from a dark colour to a light one, or covering a bold shade, often needs primer plus two coats. If you’re painting over a similar colour in good condition, one coat may be enough for a quick refresh.

  • Buy all the paint you need at once so the batch colour matches.
  • Keep a small amount for touch-ups, labelled with the room and date.

Don’t forget trim and doors

This calculator covers walls and, optionally, the ceiling. Skirting boards (baseboards), window frames and doors are normally painted with a different, harder-wearing finish. A quart or litre is usually enough for the trim of an average room.

Frequently asked questions

How much paint do I need for a 12 × 12 room?

With 8 ft ceilings, one door and one window, a 12 × 12 ft room has about 349 sq ft of wall. Two coats need about 2 gallons of paint at 350 sq ft per gallon.

How many square feet does a gallon of paint cover?

Most interior paint covers 350–400 square feet per gallon on smooth, primed walls. Rough surfaces use more. Check the can for the exact rate.

Should I subtract doors and windows?

Yes. The calculator removes about 20 sq ft per door and 15 sq ft per window, a standard estimate. It leads to a more accurate amount, particularly in rooms with large windows.

How many coats of paint do I need?

Two coats is the norm for a good finish and an even colour. One coat can be enough when repainting the same colour or using a paint-and-primer product on a clean wall.

Do I need to paint the ceiling separately?

Ceilings use flat ceiling paint. Use the “include ceiling” option to add the area, or calculate the ceiling on its own if you use a different paint.

Can I use litres and meters?

Yes. Switch to meters and the calculator accepts coverage in m² per liter and gives the answer in liters and 5-liter tins.

What if my room is not rectangular?

Split the room into rectangles, calculate each, and add the results. For an L-shaped room that is two calculations.
