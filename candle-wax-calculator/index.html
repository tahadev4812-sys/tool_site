How to use this calculator

  1. Choose whether you measure containers in fluid ounces or milliliters and enter the capacity of one jar.
  2. Enter how many candles you plan to pour in this batch.
  3. Select your wax type to see wax-specific fragrance advice.
  4. Set your fragrance load — 6–10% suits most waxes. Check your wax supplier’s recommended maximum.
  5. Press Calculate, then weigh the wax and fragrance oil on a scale rather than measuring by volume.

The formula

wax per candle (oz) = container fl oz × 0.86 // milliliters: fl oz = ml ÷ 29.574 fragrance (oz) = wax oz × (fragrance load% ÷ 100) total wax = wax per candle × number of candles grams = oz × 28.35 · pounds = oz ÷ 16
Worked exampleSix 8 fl oz jars: wax per candle = 8 × 0.86 = 6.88 oz, so the batch needs 41.3 oz (2.58 lb) of wax. At 8% load, fragrance = 6.88 × 0.08 = 0.55 oz per candle, or 3.3 oz (94 g) in total.

Why weigh wax instead of measuring volume?

A fluid ounce is a measure of space, not mass, so a jar’s capacity does not equal the weight of wax it holds. Different waxes have different densities, and melted wax is lighter than solid wax. Weighing on a digital scale is far more accurate than scooping flakes into a measuring cup. This calculator uses a standard conversion of 0.86 oz of wax for every fl oz of container capacity, which gives you a sensible fill with headspace.

Choosing a fragrance load

Fragrance load is the weight of fragrance oil as a percentage of wax weight. More is not always better. Too much fragrance oil can make candles sweat, produce a weaker flame, leave oily pools or even be a fire hazard. Most soy, paraffin and coconut waxes work best at 6–10%; beeswax is lower. Always follow the maximum load given by your wax supplier and the flashpoint of your fragrance oil.

Cure time and testing

Natural waxes such as soy and coconut need time for the fragrance to bind with the wax. Let candles cure for 1–2 weeks before testing the scent throw, and always burn-test a sample of each new combination of wax, wick, container and fragrance before you sell or gift them.

Frequently asked questions

How much wax do I need for an 8 oz candle jar?

About 6.9 oz of wax by weight. The calculator multiplies the container’s fluid-ounce capacity by 0.86 to leave sensible room below the rim.

What is a good fragrance load for soy candles?

Most soy waxes work well at 6–10%. Check your wax supplier’s technical sheet for the maximum recommended load.

How do I calculate fragrance oil for a candle?

Multiply the weight of wax by the fragrance load percentage. For 10 oz of wax at 8% load, you need 10 × 0.08 = 0.8 oz of fragrance oil.

Why does the calculator warn about loads over 10%?

Most waxes cannot hold more than around 10% fragrance oil. Overloading can cause sweating, poor burning and safety problems, so always check your manufacturer’s maximum.

Should I measure wax in ounces or grams?

Whichever your scale shows. The calculator gives ounces, grams and pounds so you can use whichever is most convenient.

Does the wax type change the amount of wax?

The amount of wax per container is a standard estimate for all four wax types. The type changes the fragrance advice, because each wax holds a different amount of fragrance oil.

Can I use the calculator for tins, jars and tumblers?

Yes — any container. Enter its capacity to the rim; the 0.86 factor leaves headspace.
