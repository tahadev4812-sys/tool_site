How to use this calculator

  1. Choose the unit you measured in — inches or centimeters — and pick the shape of your piece.
  2. Enter the length and width (or diameter) of the area you plan to cover, then the depth of the pour.
  3. Select the mix ratio printed on your epoxy: most table-top and art resins are 1:1, many deep-pour systems are 2:1.
  4. Leave waste at 10% unless you have a reason to change it, then press Calculate.
  5. Buy at least the total shown, and mix the resin and hardener amounts exactly as listed.

How much extra epoxy should you mix?

Epoxy that clings to mixing cups and stir sticks is gone for good, and a flat-looking surface is rarely perfectly level. Most makers add 10% for flat, sealed surfaces such as coasters and trays, and 15–20% for porous wood, live-edge slabs, rough molds or anything you have not sealed first. Wood soaks up resin into its grain, so a seal coat before the main pour saves material and reduces bubbles.

Running short is much worse than having a little left over. A second batch poured after the first has started to gel may not bond cleanly and can leave a visible seam in clear work.

Pour depth and heat

Epoxy cures through an exothermic reaction, meaning it generates its own heat. In a thin layer the heat escapes easily. In a thick pour it builds up, which can cause yellowing, cracking, shrinkage or even smoking. Tabletop and art epoxies are usually rated for roughly 1/8 to 1/2 inch per layer, while deep-pour formulas can reach 2 inches or more. Always follow the maximum depth on your product’s data sheet and pour in several layers when the calculator shows a deeper total.

Mixing tips for clean results

  • Measure resin and hardener separately, then combine them — never eyeball the ratio.
  • Stir for the full time on the label, scraping the sides and bottom of the container.
  • Pour the mix into a second clean cup and stir again to catch unmixed resin.
  • Work at the temperature the manufacturer recommends; cold resin is thick and traps bubbles.

Frequently asked questions

How much epoxy do I need for a 1/8 inch coat on a table?

A flood coat 1/8 in thick needs about 10 fluid ounces of mixed epoxy per square foot. A 3 ft × 5 ft table (15 sq ft) therefore needs roughly 150 fl oz, or 1.2 gallons, before waste.

Is epoxy measured by volume or by weight?

It depends on the brand. Many 1:1 and 2:1 resins are mixed by volume, but some systems specify weight. Check the label — the calculator lists both volume and an approximate weight.

Why do I need extra epoxy for waste?

Some resin always stays in the mixing cup, runs over edges or soaks into porous surfaces. A 10% buffer prevents a partial pour, which is hard to fix.

How do I convert cubic inches to gallons?

There are 231 cubic inches in one US gallon, so divide your volume in cubic inches by 231. For fluid ounces, divide by 1.805.

Can I use this calculator for molds?

Yes, for simple rectangular or round molds. For irregular shapes, fill the mold with water, pour the water into a measuring jug and use that volume instead.

How deep can I pour epoxy at once?

Standard table-top epoxy is usually limited to around 1/8 to 1/2 inch per pour. Deep-pour epoxy is formulated for thicker layers, often up to 2 inches. Never exceed the depth stated by the manufacturer.

Does the calculator work in metric units?

Yes. Switch the unit to centimeters and the calculator converts cm³ to cubic inches, then reports gallons, ounces, liters and milliliters together.
