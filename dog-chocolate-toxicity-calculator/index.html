How to use this calculator

  1. Choose lb and oz or kg and grams and enter your dog’s weight.
  2. Select the type of chocolate. Darker and more bitter means more dangerous.
  3. Enter the amount eaten (the weight of the chocolate, not the whole wrapper).
  4. Press Check risk to see the estimated theobromine dose and risk level.
  5. Call a vet or poison hotline regardless of the result. This tool is not a diagnosis.

The formula

theobromine (mg) = ounces eaten × mg per ounce dose (mg/kg) = theobromine (mg) ÷ dog weight (kg) mg per ounce (approx.): milk 58 · dark/semisweet 138 baking 390 · cocoa powder 400–737 mild signs ≥ 20 mg/kg · severe ≥ 40–50 · seizures ≥ 60
Worked exampleA 20 lb dog (9.1 kg) eats 2 oz of dark chocolate: 2 × 138 = 276 mg of theobromine. 276 ÷ 9.1 = 30 mg/kg — in the range where mild to moderate signs can appear, so contact a vet.

Why chocolate is dangerous for dogs

Chocolate contains theobromine and caffeine, stimulants that dogs process very slowly. The longer they remain in the body, the more they affect the heart, nervous system and kidneys. The risk depends on three things: the type of chocolate (darker means more theobromine), the amount, and the size of the dog. A small dog that eats one square of baking chocolate can be in more danger than a large dog that eats a whole milk chocolate bar.

What to do if your dog ate chocolate

  1. Remove any remaining chocolate and wrappers.
  2. Note the type, how much was eaten and roughly when.
  3. Call your vet or a poison helpline immediately; don’t wait for symptoms, which can take 6–12 hours.
  4. Don’t induce vomiting unless a vet instructs you to.

Other foods that are toxic

Chocolate is not the only danger. Grapes and raisins, xylitol (a sweetener in sugar-free gum and some peanut butters), onions, garlic and macadamia nuts can all be harmful. Keep these out of reach, especially at Easter, Halloween and Christmas.

Frequently asked questions

How much chocolate is toxic to a dog?

Mild signs can appear at about 20 mg/kg of theobromine, severe signs at 40–50 mg/kg, and seizures from about 60 mg/kg. For a 20 lb dog, that is roughly 1.3 oz of dark chocolate for mild signs.

My dog ate a small piece of milk chocolate. Should I worry?

Milk chocolate has less theobromine, so a small piece is often low-risk for large dogs, but small dogs, puppies and dogs with heart problems can still be affected. Call your vet for advice.

What are the symptoms of chocolate poisoning in dogs?

Vomiting, diarrhea, excessive thirst, restlessness, rapid breathing, a racing heart, muscle tremors and seizures. Signs usually appear within 6–12 hours.

Is white chocolate safe for dogs?

It contains very little theobromine, but the fat and sugar can cause vomiting or pancreatitis. It is not recommended.

Which phone number should I call?

Your own vet or nearest emergency clinic first. You can also call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435 or the Pet Poison Helpline at (855) 764-7661; fees may apply.

Is this calculator a substitute for a vet?

No. It gives a rough estimate only. Chocolate varies by brand, and the health of your dog matters too. Always seek professional advice.

What about cocoa powder and baked goods?

Cocoa powder is the most concentrated source. For baked goods such as brownies, estimate the amount of chocolate or cocoa in them and ask your vet.
